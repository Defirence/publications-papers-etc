Extreme Systemic Failure is a critical phase transition that occurs when the quantum coherence time ($\tau_c$) drops below the biological processing time ($T_{bio}$). The time for the system to lose coherence is determined by environmental factors and the inherent complexity, or effective mass ($m$), of the superposed biological components:

$$\tau_c \approx \frac{\hbar}{E_G} < T_{bio}$$

Where $E_G \propto m$ is the gravitational self-energy of the quantum superposition. This aligns directly with **Penrose's Objective Reduction (OR)** hypothesis. In this context, the "effective mass" ($m$) is not merely physical weight, but the collective *complexity size* of the superposed biological network attempting to maintain coherence. When this inequality holds, non-local coherence breaks down, and the system loses correlation, collapsing into classical thermodynamic decay (Death).
